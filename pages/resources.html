How to Get Access

The first three you sign up for yourself on your school-issued email, with no district approval needed. Canva is open only to teaching roles, so check its eligibility list first. The last two come with your district's Microsoft or Google account, so there is nothing to sign up for: you only check whether your district has them turned on. Verified against official sources in November 2026. Terms, dates, and verification partners can change.

Claude for Teachers

Claude for Teachers is free for verified US K-12 educators and includes Pro-level features. It launched in July 2026.

Before you start: the Claude account you verify has to use your school-issued email, not a personal one. If your account currently uses a personal email, start a fresh one on your school address, since this cannot be fixed by editing an account later.

Eligibility, quoted directly from Anthropic's support article:

"Claude for Teachers is for individual educators working in US K-12 schools: Classroom teachers, in any subject and any grade from kindergarten through 12th; Instructional coaches, specialists, and interventionists; Librarians, counselors, and other certificated staff."

"Important: Claude for Teachers is not for students. It's an educator-only offering."

What to have ready, quoted directly from the verification modal at claude.ai/teachers:

"Your school (US K-12, public or private). A Claude account that uses your school-issued email. One document showing your role, like a school ID, contract, pay stub, or certificate."

The steps:

  1. Go to claude.ai/teachers.
  2. Click Get started.
  3. Accept the K-12 Terms of Service and the Student Data Processing Addendum.
  4. Select your school.
  5. Upload one role document (school ID, contract, pay stub, or certificate).
  6. Wait for review.

About that review: Anthropic hands verification to a partner called Goodstack, so being sent to an unfamiliar site that asks for a pay stub is expected, not a phishing attempt. Quoted directly from the modal: "Reviews are handled by Anthropic's verification partner, Goodstack, and most finish within 24 hours, though they can take up to 3 days during busy periods." Start well before a date you actually need the account, not the night before.

Cost, quoted directly and confirmed on three official Anthropic sources: "Sign up by June 30, 2027 for your full year of free access." Your free year runs one year from the day you sign up, so signing up early buys no extra free time: start when you are ready to use it, since the clock begins that day. Just be sure to sign up on or before June 30, 2027, which is the last day to start.

Accepting the K-12 Terms of Service and Student Data Processing Addendum puts your account under a different agreement than consumer Claude, one written for work with student data. Training is off by default for every verified educator account.

ChatGPT for Teachers

Eligibility, quoted directly from OpenAI's help article: "You are eligible if you: Work for an accredited U.S. K–12 school or district, and Are a teacher, staff member, school leader, or district administrator".

Verification for ChatGPT for Teachers is handled by a different partner than Claude's: SheerID.

The steps:

  1. Go to chatgpt.com/k12-verification and verify using your school or district email.
  2. Create or join a workspace tied to your district's domain.
  3. Set your role, subjects, and grade levels.

Cost: free through June 2027. This is a fixed end date for everyone, not tied to your signup date. OpenAI has not published which day in June its free access ends, so no specific day is stated here.

ChatGPT for Teachers is not ChatGPT Plus and not ChatGPT Pro. It is a distinct workspace product built for school and district use.

Khanmigo for Teachers

The lowest-friction option here. There is no document to upload, no school email requirement, and no waiting on a review.

Who qualifies. The Khanmigo site's wording is broad: "To access Khanmigo for Teachers, you must be an educator who is 18 years or older and lives in one of these countries or territories." The terms you accept at signup are narrower: you must be "employed in an official teaching or administrative capacity by a school or other educational agency." Nobody checks this; you confirm it yourself by accepting the terms, so read them and make sure your role fits.

The steps:

  1. Go to khanmigo.ai and click "Sign up for free" under the teacher option.
  2. Create a free Khan Academy account, or log in to one you already have. Its language has to be set to English.
  3. Fill in your details and accept the terms. You are sent to Khanmigo, and a confirmation email follows.

You need to be in the US (Khan Academy checks your connection's location) or one of its other supported countries.

Cost: free, with no end date announced. Khanmigo costs $4/month or $44/year for learners and parents; the teacher tools are the free tier.

Two honest caveats. It is teacher-facing only: you cannot grant students access from a free account, since classroom access is available only through school or district implementations. And the free tools are built around lesson planning and classroom instruction, so how much of it earns its keep for pathway and FAFSA work is genuinely up to you.

Canva Education

Free premium Canva for K-12, and the quickest of any tool here if your school email domain is recognised: access is granted on the spot, with no document and no review.

Check your role first. Canva's free education plan is for people in teaching roles: certified K-12 teachers currently teaching, school librarians, learning support assistants or teachers, curriculum specialists, and certified teachers at technical or vocational schools. Counselors and advisors are not on that list. If that leaves you out, ask whether your school or district has rolled Canva Education out to its staff, since schools and districts can sign up as organizations.

The steps:

  1. Go to canva.com/education and sign up using your school-issued email, or your school's Google, Microsoft, or Clever account.
  2. Choose Teacher, then fill in your school's name, address, and website.
  3. If your school's email domain is recognised, you are in immediately. Canva's wording: "If you signed up with a verified educational email domain, you'll get access right away." It may still ask you to verify your teaching credentials later.
  4. If it is not recognised, you upload a document and Canva reviews it within 48 hours. In the US, a copy of your teaching license is all it asks for.

Cost: free, for as long as you stay eligible. Canva asks you to re-verify every three years.

Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat

If your school email is a Microsoft account (you sign in to Outlook or Teams with it), you may already have this. Microsoft's wording: "Use secure AI chat powered by GPT-5 at no additional cost when signed in with your school or work account," for schools on its A1, A3, or A5 licenses. There is nothing to sign up for.

How to check:

  1. Go to m365copilot.com and sign in with your school email, not a personal Microsoft account.
  2. Look for a green shield at the top of the chat, next to New Chat. Microsoft uses it to show that enterprise data protection applies, which means you are in your school account and not the consumer version.
  3. If you cannot sign in, or the shield is missing, your district may not have it set up. That is a district setting, so ask your district IT rather than trying to fix it yourself.

Cost: free with your district's license. The full Microsoft 365 Copilot, which works inside Word, Outlook, and Teams, is a separate paid add-on that districts buy ($18 per user per month for schools).

Gemini for Education

If your school email is a Google account (you use Gmail, Drive, or Classroom with it), you may already have Gemini. Google includes the Gemini app as a core service in its Workspace for Education editions, including the free Education Fundamentals edition. Google's wording: "By default, the Gemini app is available to all users over age 18." Your district can turn it off.

How to check:

  1. Go to gemini.google.com and sign in with your school email, not a personal Gmail.
  2. If a chat opens, you have it. Check that the account shown in the top-right corner is your school one.
  3. If Gemini will not open for that account, your district has either turned it off or not marked your account as 18 or older. Both are district settings, so ask your district IT.

Gemini in Classroom, the teacher tools inside Google Classroom, follows the same rule: on by default for users 18 and older unless the district switches it off.

Cost: free with your district's Google Workspace for Education account.
