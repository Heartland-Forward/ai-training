How to Get Access

As a rootEd advisor with a school-issued email, you can verify for two free teacher offers yourself, no district approval needed. Both offers below were verified against official sources in July 2026. Terms, dates, and verification partners can change.

Two offers, two different deadlines

These two deadlines look alike and are not. Mixing them up is the single most common mistake a reader can make on this page.

Claude for Teachers ChatGPT for Teachers
Shape Signup deadline Fixed end date
Date Sign up by June 30, 2027 Free through June 2027
What you get One year free from your signup date Free until the cliff, whenever you joined
Day published? Yes No

Claude's deadline is about when you sign up. Sign up by June 30, 2027 and your free year starts counting from your signup date, not from that deadline. Signing up earlier does not waste anything: it just moves your personal one-year clock earlier too.

ChatGPT's deadline is a fixed cliff. Free access for everyone stops sometime in June 2027, regardless of when an account was verified. OpenAI has not published which day in June, so do not treat "June 30" as ChatGPT's date. That specific date belongs to Claude.

Claude for Teachers

Claude for Teachers is free for verified US K-12 educators and includes Pro-level features. It launched in July 2026.

Before you start: the Claude account you verify has to use your school-issued email, not a personal one. This is the step people get wrong, and you cannot fix it later by editing an existing account. If your Claude account currently uses a personal email, verify with a fresh account on your school address instead.

Eligibility, quoted directly from Anthropic's support article:

"Claude for Teachers is for individual educators working in US K-12 schools: Classroom teachers, in any subject and any grade from kindergarten through 12th; Instructional coaches, specialists, and interventionists; Librarians, counselors, and other certificated staff."

"Important: Claude for Teachers is not for students. It's an educator-only offering."

What to have ready, quoted directly from the verification modal at claude.ai/teachers:

"Your school (US K-12, public or private). A Claude account that uses your school-issued email. One document showing your role, like a school ID, contract, pay stub, or certificate."

The steps:

  1. Go to claude.ai/teachers.
  2. Click Get started.
  3. Accept the K-12 Terms of Service and the Student Data Processing Addendum.
  4. Select your school.
  5. Upload one role document (school ID, contract, pay stub, or certificate).
  6. Wait for review.

About that review: Anthropic hands verification to a partner called Goodstack. If you are redirected to a site you have not heard of and it asks for something like a pay stub, that is expected, not a phishing attempt. Quoted directly from the verification modal: "Reviews are handled by Anthropic's verification partner, Goodstack, and most finish within 24 hours, though they can take up to 3 days during busy periods."

Do this now, not the night before you need it. Most reviews finish within 24 hours, but they can take up to 3 days during busy periods. Start your verification well before a date you actually need the account working.

Cost, quoted directly and confirmed on three official Anthropic sources: "Sign up by June 30, 2027 for your full year of free access." Your free year runs one year from the day you sign up, so signing up earlier does not shorten it: it just starts your clock sooner.

Accepting the K-12 Terms of Service and Student Data Processing Addendum puts your account under a different agreement than consumer Claude, one written for work with student data. Training is off by default for every verified educator account.

ChatGPT for Teachers

Eligibility, quoted directly from OpenAI's help article: "You are eligible if you: Work for an accredited U.S. K–12 school or district, and Are a teacher, staff member, school leader, or district administrator".

Verification for ChatGPT for Teachers is handled by a different partner than Claude's: SheerID.

The steps:

  1. Go to chatgpt.com/k12-verification and verify using your school or district email.
  2. Create or join a workspace tied to your district's domain.
  3. Set your role, subjects, and grade levels.

Cost: free through June 2027. This is a fixed end date for everyone, not tied to your signup date. OpenAI has not published which day in June its free access ends, so no specific day is stated here.

ChatGPT for Teachers is not ChatGPT Plus and not ChatGPT Pro. It is a distinct workspace product built for school and district use.

Khanmigo for Teachers

The lowest-friction of the three. There is no verification step at all: no document, no school email requirement, no waiting on a review.

Who qualifies. Khan Academy's wording is broad: "To access Khanmigo for Teachers, you must be an educator who is 18 years or older and lives in one of these countries or territories." It never defines "educator," and asks for no proof of one.

The steps:

  1. Create a free Khan Academy account, or sign in to one you already have.
  2. Go to khanmigo.ai and choose the free teacher tools.
  3. That is the whole flow. There is nothing to verify and nobody to wait on.

Cost: free, with no end date. Khanmigo costs $4/mo for learners and parents; the teacher tools are the free tier.

Two honest caveats. It is teacher-facing only: you cannot grant students access from a free account, since classroom access is available only through school or district implementations. And the free tools are built around lesson planning and classroom instruction, so how much of it earns its keep for pathway and FAFSA work is genuinely up to you.

Free for teachers, but not for you

These three are genuinely free for K-12, and you will see them on every "free AI tools for educators" list. They are here because their eligibility does not reach a rootEd advisor, and it is better to know that now than after you have spent an afternoon on an application. Worth passing to the teachers you work with, who do qualify.

Canva Education

Free for eligible teachers and students, K-12 only. All five eligible roles begin with the word "certified": a certified teacher, librarian, learning support assistant, or curriculum specialist. The signup wall asks you to "Verify you're an active school teacher," and US applicants "only need to provide a copy of their teaching license."

An advisor appears on neither Canva's eligible nor its ineligible list, so this is a role Canva has not addressed rather than one it names and excludes. Applying as an individual means confirming you are a teacher, which you are not.

One wrinkle worth knowing, since it looks like an opening and mostly is not. Canva's approved documents include "a photo or scan showing employment status at a school," which an advisor could produce. But that list is headed "Official teacher identification that shows current status," and Canva's own note says applicants from the United States "only need to provide a copy of their teaching license." A verified school email domain also grants access immediately with no document at all, which is how an advisor would get in without ever being asked the question directly.

There is a second path worth knowing. Canva also grants Education access to organizations, naming "K-12 schools, school districts, departments of education" among them. If your district already holds Canva Education, you may be covered by that rather than needing to qualify yourself. Ask whoever administers it before applying on your own. Note that all Canva Education users re-verify every three years.

Figma for Education

Figma is the clearest of the three, and the clearest is a no. Verbatim: "If you are a staff member at a school in a non-teaching role, you do not qualify." No inference needed.

Two things worth knowing even for the teachers you pass this to. K-12 accounts get no AI at all: "AI tools are not available for K-12 Education users." And education status is tied to the email you verify with, so changing that email later revokes it.

GitHub Copilot Pro

The requirement reads broader than Canva's: "Be an educator, faculty member, or researcher," plus a school-issued email and uploaded proof of school affiliation. That foregrounds employment rather than a credential, and an employment verification letter is something a district HR office can issue to a non-teaching employee.

But the application itself is narrower than the requirement. GitHub's own description of the teacher route: "Educators can join GitHub Education by verifying their teacher status with a school-issued email and documentation of school affiliation," and the form asks you to select Teacher. Teacher status is the thing being verified, and a human reviews it.

Your school is not the obstacle, despite how it looks: GitHub's school program asks for "an educational institution offering degrees or certificates," and a high school grants both. GitHub does not publish how long review takes, so do not count on a turnaround, and it reevaluates eligibility monthly. Fair warning that it is also a coding tool, which is a stretch for pathway and FAFSA work.
